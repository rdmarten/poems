Memories of West Lethbridge

for my brother, D.W.J.

I could say it is:

the orange-blue flame
of a BIC lighter
in the dark evenings
on the snowy steps
of your modest house

the raucous laughter
of Maskandi singer smiles
in the dark evenings
on a dirty brown settee
of your 70s basement

“so much
has happened
since painting,
do you
not agree?”

the white quietness
of the Old Man River
in the bright mornings
on an overgrown path
of your old hometown

the fireball whiskey
of the local owlery
in the bright mornings
on a vintage record player
of your parquet living room

“it is
the second-best
dog-sledge race,
I swear
on paper”

the off-key piano
of shared Joni’s melodies
in the dusky afternoon
on a cracking bench
of your splintered sunroom




I should say it is:

wherever
we sit
to harmonise
to breathe
to listen

to the snowflakes
fall and

you are
my home
