Encrypt a poem

Turns a poem into a page that only opens with a passphrase.

This runs entirely in your browser. The poem is encrypted with AES-GCM using a key derived from your passphrase (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 iterations). What you save into the repo is ciphertext: no plaintext, and no passphrase. Anyone who views the source of the published page sees a base64 blob and the routine that decrypts it, which tells them nothing.

Two things this cannot do for you. The ciphertext is public, so anyone can guess at it offline as fast as their hardware allows — your passphrase is the entire security model. Use the generator below rather than inventing one. And encryption only protects text that was never committed in the clear: draft in drafts/ (already git-ignored), never in a poem folder.

2. The passphrase

You can type your own, though the generator below is much safer. Either way, write it down somewhere safe before you leave this page: there is no way to recover it, and no way to decrypt the poem without it.

3. The page

Save this as <poem-name>/index.html in the repo, then commit and push. Test it in a browser before you share the link.

Each build makes a fresh random salt and IV, so re-encrypting the same poem with the same passphrase produces a different blob. That is expected.
