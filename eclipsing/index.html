eclipsing

for E.P.

On the other side of Manchester, I know you are watching the eclipse. One flight of the crow away. Halation might occur, you taught me. I can’t look for it; I don’t have the proper glasses.

A stream of implicit liquid trickled down from the back of the bus. Slightly concerned heads turned cautiously to look for its source. It’s too warm not to be suspicious.

Markings on the road. The song in my headphones hits C Major; I smile with some proleptic nostalgia. I imagine how the humid winds go through your shirt, feeling for your heart.

Cosmic thinking requires bravery, my courage. Its scale terrifies. All that space, dauntingly just 9.917 kilometres to the north. I could cycle it in under 50 minutes, and you’d laugh.

It is 6.44 pm, and paths are aligning. 88.6%. It feels more. A pair of red-rimmed eclipse glasses is tucked away in one of my unpacked boxes. I got them from a pharmacy ages ago.

Though I am not really supposed to, I stare up. Careful, blond blindness might set in, you’d joke. I’m unprepared for any of this. The sun always uxoriously rises, I’d declare.

Curly curved clouds. A little rat crosses the field. Always comes back to the same spot. It’s nice to think they will be around for the next, I’d joke. Good cosmic thinking, you’d quip.

People are starting to disperse at 7.09 pm. I’m still sat here, 9.917 kilometres to the south. The alignment should soon start to peak. 91.4%. It’ll grow deeper. Picnickers among the daisies.

At some point, a dream: I go home and hear children playing in the park. Smile with some analeptic nostalgia. We sit down, fully prepared, without glasses. Boxes all unpacked. Close enough.
